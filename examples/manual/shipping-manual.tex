\chapter{Introduction}

\section{What this plugin does}

Riverbend Shipping replaces WooCommerce's flat-rate shipping with a calculator built
around how an aquatics shop actually dispatches an order. It knows the difference
between a bag of shrimp, a glass aquarium and a tube of glue, and prices each of
them the way Riverbend Aquatics prices them today.

For any given cart it works out:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dry-goods delivery} --- one flat charge for the ordinary items in the order.
  \item \textbf{The very-large-item charge} --- charged per very-large item, \emph{instead of}
        the dry-goods rate, not in addition to it.
  \item \textbf{Livestock delivery} --- at a local or a national rate, depending on the
        delivery postcode.
  \item \textbf{Cooler boxes} --- how many insulated boxes the livestock needs, from the
        number and size of packing bags, and what they cost.
  \item \textbf{Heat packs} --- one per cooler box, but only while heat-pack season is on.
  \item \textbf{The free-shipping discount} --- whether the order has earned free dry-goods
        delivery, and what that is worth.
  \item \textbf{Collection} --- an order collected from the Riverbend store pays no
        transport, but still pays for its packaging.
\end{itemize}

Every one of those components is stored on the order and shown to the customer, so
the shipping figure on an invoice can always be explained.

\section{What it does not do}

\begin{itemize}
  \item It does not calculate VAT. Every rate you enter is \emph{excluding} VAT, and
        WooCommerce adds tax according to your own tax settings.
  \item It does not block checkout on non-dispatch days. It shows the dispatch
        information and lets the customer decide.
  \item It does not price an outlying delivery. It charges the national rate, flags the
        order, and leaves the surcharge to a conversation with the customer.
  \item It does not reclassify your catalogue. A product with no shipping class keeps
        being charged the ordinary dry-goods rate, exactly as it is today.
\end{itemize}

\section{How it is put together}

The plugin is two layers, and the split is worth knowing about because it explains
where to look when something needs changing.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.2cm}L{10.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{What lives there}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{The engine}\newline \opt{includes/} &
Every pricing rule. Contains no WordPress code at all --- no options, no database, no
clock. It takes a cart plus a set of settings and returns a priced breakdown. This is
what makes the pricing testable without a shop.\\
\addlinespace
\textbf{The adapter}\newline \opt{wordpress/} &
The only code that knows what WooCommerce is. It gathers the cart, hands it to the
engine, and puts the answer back where WooCommerce expects it --- as a shipping rate,
as cart fees, and as order meta.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\begin{houseinfo}
The practical consequence: changing a \emph{price} is a settings change in wp-admin.
Changing a \emph{rule} is a change to the engine. Nothing about the pricing is hidden
in a theme or a template.
\end{houseinfo}

\chapter{Installation and first-time setup}

\section{Requirements}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.5cm}L{10.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Requirement} & \textbf{Minimum}\\
\midrule
\endhead
WordPress & 6.4\\
WooCommerce & 8.0\\
PHP & 8.2\\
Order storage & Works with both legacy post storage and High-Performance Order Storage (HPOS).\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The plugin has \textbf{no runtime dependencies}. There is nothing to build, no
Composer install to run on the server, and no \opt{vendor} folder in the distributed
package.

\section{Installing the plugin}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item In wp-admin, go to \menu{Plugins $\rightarrow$ Add New $\rightarrow$ Upload Plugin}.
  \item Choose \opt{riverbend-shipping-0.1.0.zip} and select \menu{Install Now}.
  \item Select \menu{Activate Plugin}.
\end{enumerate}

Activation seeds every setting with its default value, so the settings screen shows
real numbers immediately rather than a page of empty fields. Seeding never overwrites
a value that already exists, so deactivating and reactivating the plugin will not
undo your configuration.

\begin{housewarn}[Test on a staging site first]
The plugin changes what customers are charged and what delivery options they are
offered. Install it on a staging copy of the live site, work through
Chapter~\ref{ch:calc} against real products, and only then move it to production.
\end{housewarn}

\section{Adding it to a shipping zone}

Installing the plugin does not by itself put it in front of a customer. WooCommerce
only offers a shipping method that has been added to a zone.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Go to \menu{WooCommerce $\rightarrow$ Settings $\rightarrow$ Shipping $\rightarrow$ Zones}.
  \item Open the zone that covers the country (or create one).
  \item Select \menu{Add shipping method} and choose \menu{Riverbend Shipping}.
  \item Save.
\end{enumerate}

Repeat for every zone that should use these rates. If a zone has other methods on it
(Flat rate, Free shipping, Local pickup), the customer will see those alongside
Riverbend Shipping --- see Section~\ref{sec:collection-clash} about Local Pickup in
particular.

\section{First-run checklist}

Work through this list before the plugin goes anywhere near a real customer.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.6cm}L{9.6cm}L{4.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{Check} & \textbf{Where}\\
\midrule
\endhead
$\square$ & Every rate is entered \emph{excluding} VAT. & Settings, Shipping rates\\
$\square$ & The free-shipping threshold and its exclusion list are right. & Settings, Free shipping\\
$\square$ & Cooler-box capacity and charges match what the shop actually does. & Settings, Cooler boxes\\
$\square$ & Heat-pack season is set to the correct mode and state. & Settings, Cooler boxes\\
$\square$ & The local-delivery postcode list has been corrected. & Settings, Delivery areas\\
$\square$ & Every shipping-class slug in the translation list actually exists. & \menu{Products $\rightarrow$ Shipping classes}\\
$\square$ & Livestock products have a units-per-bag and a bag size set. & Product edit / bulk edit\\
$\square$ & Free-shipping exclusions are ticked on the products that need them. & Product edit / bulk edit\\
$\square$ & The four customer notices read the way you want them to. & Settings, Checkout notices\\
$\square$ & A test order has been placed and its breakdown checked. & Front end\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\begin{housewarn}[The VAT trap]
Every rate on this screen is \emph{excluding} VAT. A shop whose published prices
include it has to convert before typing. Enter \opt{160} where \R{160} was meant to
be the inclusive figure, with shipping set to taxable, and every customer immediately
starts paying \R{184.00} instead. Decide which of the two figures you are entering
before you type it, not afterwards.
\end{housewarn}

\chapter{Settings reference}

Every setting lives in one place:

\begin{center}
\menu{WooCommerce $\rightarrow$ Settings $\rightarrow$ Shipping $\rightarrow$ Riverbend Shipping}
\end{center}

There is no separate admin menu for this plugin. The screen is built with
WooCommerce's own settings system, so it looks and saves like the rest of the store.

Settings are stored one row per value, each key prefixed \opt{riverbend\_shipping\_}. A
single bad value therefore cannot make the rest unreadable. If the saved settings
cannot be validated at all, the plugin falls back to the shipped defaults and writes
a warning to the log rather than breaking a checkout.

\section{Free shipping}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{5.0cm}L{2.6cm}L{7.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Default} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Free dry-goods shipping from & \R{2\,500.00} & Measured on the merchandise value \emph{before} coupon discounts, excluding shipping and packaging.\\
\addlinespace
An order exactly on the threshold qualifies & \yes & Ticked: \R{2\,500.00} exactly qualifies. Unticked: the order must exceed it.\\
\addlinespace
Shipping types that block free shipping & all five non-dry-goods types & Any of these anywhere in the cart disqualifies the whole order, however much it is worth.\\
\addlinespace

\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The five types selected by default are: \emph{Very large dry goods}, \emph{Livestock
and plants}, \emph{Collection only}, \emph{Shipping quote required} and
\emph{Livestock shipping unavailable}. In other words, only an order made entirely of
ordinary dry goods can earn free shipping.

\begin{housewarn}[The weight rule fires unevenly]
The weight exclusion can only act on products that actually carry a weight. On a
catalogue where weights are patchy it therefore applies to some heavy items and not
others, which looks arbitrary from the outside. It is a setting rather than a fixed
rule precisely so that keeping it, or switching it off with \opt{0}, is a deliberate
decision rather than an accident.
\end{housewarn}

\endhead

\addlinespace

\addlinespace
Charge per cooler box & \R{60.00} & What the customer pays per box.\\
\addlinespace
Charge per heat pack & \R{25.00} & What the customer pays per heat pack.\\

\addlinespace

\addlinespace

\end{longtable}

\subsection{The two season modes}

\textbf{By hand} is the default and nothing turns itself on. You tick \emph{Heat-pack
season is on now} when winter starts and untick it when it ends. The start and end
date fields are ignored.

\textbf{Between two dates} compares month and day only, so a window set once keeps
working every year. A window that runs backwards --- for example \opt{11-01} to
\opt{02-28} --- correctly wraps across the new year rather than matching nothing.

The date is the shop's own date, not the server's, so a local winter setting
does not switch over at UTC midnight.

\section{Delivery areas}

Postcodes may be written one per line or separated by commas or semicolons. Ranges
work: \opt{2400-2599}. Spaces around the dash are fine. Anything not listed is
treated as elsewhere in the country.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.4cm}L{4.4cm}L{5.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Default} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Local delivery postcodes & \opt{2100-2160, 2400-2599,}\newline\opt{2700-2999, 3000-3099} & Gets the local livestock rate.\\
\addlinespace
Outlying postcodes & empty & Priced at the national rate, but the order is flagged so an additional fee can be confirmed with the customer before dispatch.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\begin{housewarn}[The shipped postcodes are a starting point, not an answer]
The default list is a rough boundary around the Riverbend metro: the coastal strip,
the northern suburbs, the southern line and the city centre. It ships as a placeholder
so the setting is never empty, and it \textbf{needs checking against the areas the
shop actually drives to} before the plugin goes live. A postcode in the list is a
promise to deliver there at the local rate.
\end{housewarn}

A postcode cannot be on both lists. The settings screen refuses to save an overlap
and tells you which entries clash.

\section{Product shipping types}
\label{sec:classmap}

This is where your WooCommerce shipping classes are translated into the meanings the
calculator understands. One rule per line, written as \opt{class-slug = meaning}.
Blank lines and lines starting with \opt{\#} are ignored.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.6cm}L{10.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Meaning} & \textbf{What it does}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\opt{dry\_goods} & Ordinary item. Goes in the dry-goods bucket at the flat rate.\\
\opt{very\_large} & Charged the very-large rate per item, instead of the dry-goods rate.\\
\opt{livestock} & Livestock or plants. Needs a bag count; the bag size comes from the product.\\
\opt{livestock\_standard} & Livestock, and the class itself says standard packing bag.\\
\opt{livestock\_large} & Livestock, and the class itself says large packing bag.\\
\opt{collection\_only} & Cannot be couriered. Withholds the courier option for the whole order.\\
\opt{quote\_required} & Priced as normal, but the customer is told a quotation may be needed.\\
\opt{livestock\_unavailable} & Livestock that cannot currently be shipped at all. Withholds the courier option.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The shipped default covers both the terms the catalogue uses today and the seven
target terms for when it is rebuilt:

\begin{lstlisting}
# Live terms, as the catalogue stands today.
livestock = livestock
large-hevy-items = very_large
tanks-or-bubble-wrap-items = very_large
normal-products = dry_goods
local-pickup = collection_only

# The seven target terms, for when the catalogue is rebuilt.
normal-dry-goods = dry_goods
very-large-dry-goods = very_large
livestock-plants-standard-packing-bag = livestock_standard
livestock-large-packing-bag = livestock_large
collection-only = collection_only
shipping-quote-required = quote_required
livestock-shipping-unavailable = livestock_unavailable
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Products with no shipping class} (default \opt{dry\_goods}) decides what
happens to a product that has no shipping class set at all. On most catalogues that
is the majority of products, so the default matters: leaving it on \opt{dry\_goods}
means installing the plugin does not silently reprice thousands of items on day one.

\begin{housewarn}[Verify your slugs]
A slug that does not match any real shipping class is not an error --- those products
just quietly fall through to the unclassified default. Check the actual slugs under
\menu{Products $\rightarrow$ Shipping classes} and correct the list. It is a
two-minute job and it is the single most likely cause of a product being priced
wrongly.
\end{housewarn}

\chapter{Setting up products}

\section{The three things a product needs}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A WooCommerce shipping class}, which the translation list
        (Section~\ref{sec:classmap}) turns into a meaning. This is WooCommerce's own
        field, not something the plugin adds.
  \item \textbf{Packing detail}, for livestock only: how many share a bag, and what size
        bag.
  \item \textbf{A free-shipping exclusion flag}, for the products that should never earn
        free delivery regardless of order value.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The fields the plugin adds}

On a product, these appear under \menu{Product data $\rightarrow$ Shipping}.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.2cm}L{2.8cm}L{7.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Applies to} & \textbf{What it means}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Units per packing bag & Livestock only & How many of this product ship in one bag --- three shrimp to a bag, one koi to a bag. Leave blank for anything that is not livestock.\\
\addlinespace
Packing bag size & Livestock only & \textsf{Standard} or \textsf{Large}. A large bag takes twice the cooler-box space of a standard one.\\
\addlinespace
Excluded from free shipping & Any product & This product never counts towards free shipping and disqualifies the whole order, whatever it is worth.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection{What happens when they are not set}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.4cm}L{10.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Missing value} & \textbf{What the plugin does}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Units per bag, on a livestock product &
Assumes \textbf{one per bag} and writes a warning to the log. This over-quotes
packaging rather than under-quoting it --- the shop is not left absorbing the
difference, but the customer may be charged for more boxes than the order needs.\\
\addlinespace
Bag size, on a livestock product &
Uses the size implied by the shipping class if there is one; otherwise assumes
\textbf{standard} and writes a warning.\\
\addlinespace
Shipping class, on any product &
Uses whatever \textbf{Products with no shipping class} is set to --- \opt{dry\_goods}
by default.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\begin{houseinfo}[Variations]
Variations have their own \emph{Units per packing bag} and \emph{Packing bag size}
fields, under the variation's dimensions. Leaving a variation's field blank makes it
fall back to the parent product's value, so a value set once on the parent applies to
every variation that does not override it.
\end{houseinfo}

\section{Bulk editing}

Setting units-per-bag one product at a time on a catalogue of several thousand items
is not a task anybody finishes. All three fields are therefore available in
WooCommerce's bulk edit:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Go to \menu{Products}, filter to the group you want (by category, or by
        shipping class).
  \item Tick the products, choose \menu{Bulk actions $\rightarrow$ Edit}, and select
        \menu{Apply}.
  \item The three fields appear at the bottom of the bulk-edit panel.
\end{enumerate}

Every field defaults to \textsf{--- No change ---}, so selecting four hundred products
to set one value cannot silently blank the other two.

\section{A practical order of work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Confirm the shipping-class slugs and fix the translation list.
  \item Bulk-set \emph{Excluded from free shipping} on the categories that need it
        (tanks, hardscape, wood, rocks, and any named exclusions).
  \item Filter to the livestock shipping class and bulk-set \emph{Packing bag size} to
        \textsf{Standard}.
  \item Filter to the large-bagged species and bulk-set them to \textsf{Large}.
  \item Bulk-set \emph{Units per packing bag} group by group --- shrimp and small
        tetras at several per bag, cichlids and koi at one.
  \item Check the log (Chapter~\ref{ch:maintenance}) for any livestock product the
        plugin had to guess at.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{How the price is calculated}
\label{ch:calc}

The calculation runs in six steps, in a fixed order. This chapter follows that order.
Everything here is deterministic: the same cart with the same settings always
produces exactly the same answer.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\section{Step 0: which area is this going to?}

Before anything is priced, the delivery postcode is classified.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{3.0cm}L{11.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Area} & \textbf{When}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{Outlying} & The postcode is on the outlying list.\\
\textbf{Local} & The postcode is on the local list.\\
\textbf{National} & Everything else --- a postcode on neither list, a blank postcode
(as on the cart page before an address is entered), or an address outside the
country.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Only livestock delivery has separate local and national rates. Outlying is priced
identically to National; the difference is that the order is flagged for a human to
confirm any additional fee.

\begin{houseinfo}
Falling back to National for an unknown postcode is the safe direction to be wrong
in. Quoting \R{280} where \R{120} was due is a refund; quoting \R{120} where \R{280}
was due is a loss the shop absorbs on every order until somebody notices.
\end{houseinfo}

\section{Step 1: sorting the cart}

Every line in the cart is put into exactly one bucket, based on its shipping class:

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{3.4cm}L{11.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Bucket} & \textbf{Contents}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Dry goods & Everything mapped to \opt{dry\_goods}. The bucket keeps a running subtotal
and a line count.\\
\addlinespace
Very large & Everything mapped to \opt{very\_large}. The bucket keeps a total
\emph{quantity}, not a line count --- two of the same aquarium is two items.\\
\addlinespace
Livestock & Everything mapped to a livestock meaning. Lines pointing at the same
product are merged and their quantities summed \emph{before} any bag arithmetic.\\
\addlinespace
Flagged & \opt{collection\_only}, \opt{quote\_required} and
\opt{livestock\_unavailable}. These never produce a charge; they carry a restriction,
not a price.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

At the same time, two figures are collected for the free-shipping decision: the
\textbf{gross merchandise value} (the sum of every line's subtotal, before coupon
discounts and excluding tax) and whether \textbf{any product carries the exclusion
flag}.

\section{Step 1a: does this order earn free shipping?}

The order qualifies only if \textbf{all three} are true:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The gross merchandise value meets the threshold. (Whether ``meets'' means
        $\geq$ or $>$ is the \emph{exactly on the threshold} setting.)
  \item No shipping class in the cart is on the blocking list.
  \item No product in the cart is individually flagged as excluded from free shipping
        --- either by its tickbox or by exceeding the weight limit.
\end{enumerate}

If it does not qualify, the plugin also works out the \emph{shortfall} --- how much
more merchandise would meet the threshold --- so the cart can tell the customer.

\section{Step 2: dry-goods delivery}

If the dry-goods bucket has anything in it, two lines are produced:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dry-goods delivery} --- the flat rate. One charge for the whole bucket,
        not per item.
  \item \textbf{Free-shipping discount} --- exactly minus the delivery charge if the
        order qualifies, otherwise \R{0.00}.
\end{itemize}

Both lines appear even when the discount is zero, because
\textsf{Free-shipping discount: R0.00} next to \textsf{Dry-goods delivery: R160.00}
tells the customer something useful --- the discount exists and this order did not
earn it.

The discount is always computed from the same delivery charge it cancels, never from
a second setting, so the two can never drift apart.

If the cart has \emph{no} dry goods in it --- a livestock-only order, say --- neither
line appears at all. There is no dry-goods delivery to charge.

\section{Step 3: very-large items}

If the very-large bucket has anything in it:

\[
\textsf{Very-large-item charge} = \textsf{very-large rate} \times \textsf{number of very-large items}
\]

The item count is recorded alongside the money, so a \R{640} line visibly comes from
two items rather than being an unexplained number.

Very-large products never enter the dry-goods bucket, so they never attract the
dry-goods rate as well. The specification says the very-large charge is used
\emph{instead of} the normal rate, and this is where that is enforced.

\section{Step 4: livestock delivery and packaging}

Two halves that run independently. The delivery rate depends only on where the order
is going; the packaging depends only on what is in it.

\subsection{Delivery}

One \textbf{Livestock delivery} charge for the whole consignment, at the local or the
national rate.

\subsection{Bags}

For each livestock product \emph{separately}:

\[
\textsf{bags} = \left\lceil \frac{\textsf{quantity}}{\textsf{units per bag}} \right\rceil
\]

The $\lceil\ \rceil$ brackets mean \emph{round up to the next whole number}. Ten fish
at three to a bag is four bags, not three and a third.

Products are counted on their own before anything is summed, because a bag never
mixes species. Four shrimp and one betta are five animals but two bags.

\subsection{Cooler boxes}

Bags from every product are then pooled, because standard and large bags \emph{may}
share a cooler box:

\[
\textsf{slots} = \textsf{standard bags} + (\textsf{large bags} \times \textsf{large-bag slots})
\]
\[
\textsf{cooler boxes} = \left\lceil \frac{\textsf{slots}}{\textsf{cooler-box capacity}} \right\rceil
\]

With the shipped defaults that is $\lceil (\textsf{standard} + \textsf{large} \times
2) / 4 \rceil$.

\subsection{Charges}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cooler-box charge} $=$ charge per box $\times$ number of boxes.
  \item \textbf{Heat-pack charge} $=$ charge per pack $\times$ number of boxes, but
        \emph{only} while heat-pack season is on. One heat pack per cooler box.
\end{itemize}

The box count and the heat-pack count are both recorded and displayed.

An order with no cooler boxes produces no packaging lines at all --- not a pair of
\R{0.00} lines. There is no packaging to account for, so there is nothing to say.

\section{Step 5: gathering the breakdown}

The three sets of charges are merged into one breakdown, in a fixed display order:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item Livestock delivery
  \item Dry-goods delivery
  \item Cooler-box charge
  \item Heat-pack charge
  \item Very-large-item charge
  \item Free-shipping discount
  \item Manual surcharge or adjustment
\end{enumerate}

Each line may appear at most once --- an attempt to add the same line twice is a hard
error rather than a silent double charge.

Any flagged items are attached at this point, and so is a manual adjustment if one
has been set (see Section~\ref{sec:manual}).

\section{Step 6: the whole-order rules}
\label{sec:step6}

Two rules that act on the order as a whole, in this order:

\textbf{1. Collection removes transport.} If the customer is collecting, every
transport line is zeroed --- livestock delivery, dry-goods delivery, the very-large
charge, \emph{and} the free-shipping discount. Packaging is untouched and stays
payable.

\begin{houseinfo}
The free-shipping discount is treated as transport for exactly this reason. If
collection zeroed the \R{160} delivery but left a $-$\R{160} discount standing, a
collected order would quote \emph{minus} \R{160} --- the shop paying the customer to
come and fetch their own order.
\end{houseinfo}

\textbf{2. If transport ended up free, packaging may follow.} If the order had
transport charges and they came to nothing, and \emph{Packaging stays payable when
transport is free} is unticked, the packaging is zeroed too. With the default setting
(ticked) this rule never fires.

A manual adjustment survives both rules, because it is neither transport nor
packaging.

\chapter{What customers and staff see}

\section{On the cart and checkout}

\subsection{The delivery options}

Up to two options are offered: the courier option and, if enabled, the collection
option. Both are labelled from the settings.

If the cart contains anything that cannot be couriered --- a collection-only item, or
livestock the shop cannot currently ship --- then the \textbf{courier option is
withheld entirely} and collection is offered even if it is normally switched off, so
the customer still has a way to complete the order. Quoting a courier price for an
order that cannot go on a courier, and discovering it after payment, is the failure
this prevents.

\subsection{The breakdown under the rate}

When the transport figure is made up of more than one component, those components are
listed under the rate at checkout. A single component is not listed, because
repeating one number under two labels explains nothing.

Zero lines are hidden, with one exception: the free-shipping discount is always shown
when it is present, because \textsf{Free-shipping discount: R0.00} is genuinely
informative.

\subsection{The packaging fees}

Cooler boxes and heat packs appear as their own cart fee lines. When there is more
than one, the count is put in the label so the customer can check the arithmetic:

\begin{center}
\textsf{Cooler-box charge (2 x R60.00)}\hspace{2em}\textsf{R120.00}
\end{center}

\subsection{The notices}

Up to four notices are shown on the cart, in this order: the dispatch-day notice
(always, on any non-empty cart), then the quotation notice, the cannot-be-couriered
notice and the outlying-address notice as they apply.

\begin{housewarn}[Notices do not show on the block cart or checkout]
\label{sec:blocknotice}
The notices are added using WooCommerce's standard notice mechanism. That works on
the \textbf{classic} shortcode cart and checkout pages. It does \textbf{not} work on
the newer \textbf{block} cart and checkout, which render their own notices and
discard informational ones added this way.

Pricing is completely unaffected --- this is display only. If the store uses block
checkout pages and the notices are needed, supporting them means shipping a small
JavaScript bundle through the WooCommerce Blocks integration API. See
Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}.
\end{housewarn}

\section{On the order}

\subsection{The customer's view}

On the order-received page and in every order email, the breakdown is inserted
directly \emph{underneath} the shipping line, where it explains the number above it.

Only components that add something are shown. Specifically:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Zero-value lines are omitted.
  \item Packaging and manual adjustments are omitted, because they are already real
        fee line items that WooCommerce prints itself --- printing them again would
        say \textsf{Cooler-box charge: R60.00} twice.
  \item If only \emph{one} transport component remains, nothing is shown at all: one
        line on its own is just WooCommerce's own \textsf{Shipping} row under a
        different name. Two or more genuinely explain how the figure was reached.
\end{itemize}

The cooler-box, heat-pack and very-large-item \emph{counts} are shown alongside.

\subsection{The shop's view}

The same breakdown appears on the order edit screen in wp-admin, under the shipping
total, plus one extra row --- \textsf{Needs attention} --- listing any flagged items.

The plugin also adds \textbf{order notes} for anything a person has to act on:

\begin{itemize}
  \item An outlying delivery address, with a reminder to confirm the additional fee
        before dispatch.
  \item A list of items needing attention before dispatch.
  \item A warning that the order contains items that cannot be couriered.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the numbers never change retrospectively}

The breakdown is written onto the order as it is created, and it is \emph{read back}
from the order rather than recalculated. An order placed in March still shows the
figures it was actually charged, not what today's settings would produce.

\chapter{Routine maintenance}
\label{ch:maintenance}

\section{Changing a rate}

Go to the settings screen, change the figure, save. That is the whole procedure.
Every rate is in exactly one place, so a rate can never drift out of step with a copy
of itself.

Existing orders are unaffected --- they keep the figures they were charged.

\begin{housewarn}
Remember the figure you type excludes VAT. If shipping is taxable on this store, the
customer pays more than the number you enter.
\end{housewarn}

\section{Turning heat-pack season on and off}

In \textbf{by hand} mode (the default): tick or untick \emph{Heat-pack season is on
now} and save. The change takes effect on the next cart calculation.

To automate it, set \emph{Heat-pack season} to \textsf{Between two dates each year}
and set the start and end to \opt{MM-DD} values. The manual tickbox is then ignored.

\section{Adjusting the local delivery area}

Edit the \emph{Local delivery postcodes} box. Use ranges wherever possible --- a
textarea holding several hundred four-digit numbers is a setting nobody maintains
correctly.

If you add a postcode that is already on the outlying list, the save is rejected and
the conflicting entries are named.

\section{Adding a new product}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Set the WooCommerce shipping class as normal.
  \item If it is livestock: set \emph{Units per packing bag} and \emph{Packing bag
        size}.
  \item If it should never earn free shipping: tick \emph{Excluded from free
        shipping}.
  \item Add it to a test cart and check the quoted figure before it goes live.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reading the log}

When the plugin has to guess at something, it says so --- without ever interrupting a
checkout. Warnings go to:

\begin{center}
\menu{WooCommerce $\rightarrow$ Status $\rightarrow$ Logs}, source \opt{riverbend-shipping}
\end{center}

Typical entries and what to do about them:

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{7.2cm}L{7.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Message} & \textbf{What to do}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textsf{``\dots'' is livestock but has no units-per-bag set. Assuming 1 per bag} &
Set \emph{Units per packing bag} on that product. Until you do, its packaging is
over-quoted.\\
\addlinespace
\textsf{``\dots'' is livestock but has no packing-bag size set} &
Set \emph{Packing bag size}, or map its shipping class to
\opt{livestock\_standard} / \opt{livestock\_large}.\\
\addlinespace
\textsf{Product \dots{} is in a cart but could not be loaded} &
The product was deleted while in somebody's cart. It was treated as dry goods.
Usually harmless.\\
\addlinespace
\textsf{Shipping settings are invalid, so the shipped defaults are being used} &
\textbf{Act on this immediately.} A setting is unreadable and the shop is quoting
default prices. Open the settings screen; it will name the problem.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Each distinct message is logged once per page load rather than once per cart line, so
the log stays readable.

\section{Checking a quote by hand}

To reproduce a figure a customer was quoted:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open the order and read the stored breakdown --- it records exactly what was
        charged.
  \item If the order looks wrong, rebuild the same cart on the front end and compare.
  \item If the two differ, a setting or a product's data has changed since the order
        was placed. The order is right; the current settings are what changed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Upgrading the plugin}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Take a backup, or work on staging first.
  \item Go to \menu{Plugins}, deactivate \menu{Riverbend Shipping} and delete it.
  \item Upload and activate the new zip.
\end{enumerate}

Deleting the plugin through wp-admin does not remove its settings or any order data,
so settings survive an upgrade. Adding a setting in a later version does not break an
existing install --- an unsaved new setting simply uses its default.

\chapter{Known limitations and open decisions}
\label{ch:limits}

Everything in this chapter works as described --- these are places where a decision
has been made on the shop's behalf and is worth confirming, or where a known gap
exists.

\section{Decisions made on your behalf}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{5.0cm}L{9.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Question} & \textbf{What the plugin does today}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Does exactly \R{2\,500} qualify for free shipping? &
Yes, inclusive. Reversible with one tickbox.\\
\addlinespace
Should packaging appear as money on the invoice? &
Yes --- two lines, \textsf{Cooler-box charge} and \textsf{Heat-pack charge}. Without
them, packaging money appears in the total with nothing accounting for it.\\
\addlinespace
Is free shipping automatic or opt-in? &
Automatic. It is applied the moment the order qualifies, rather than offered as an
option the customer has to notice and select.\\
\addlinespace
How are livestock grouped into bags? &
By product, not by species. Two different shrimp products each get their own bag, even
though a packer would combine them. Closing this needs a species field, which is a
catalogue change rather than a plugin one.\\
\addlinespace
Are the free-shipping exclusions actually enforced? &
Yes. Both the blocking classes and the per-product tickbox are hard gates, not
advisory. An order failing either one is never offered free delivery.\\
\addlinespace
The over-4\,kg exclusion. &
On by default, and adjustable. It only acts on products that carry a weight, so on a
catalogue with patchy weights it applies unevenly. Set the limit to \opt{0} to switch
the rule off entirely.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Still outstanding}

\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,font=\sffamily\bfseries]
  \item[The local-delivery postcode list.] What ships is a placeholder boundary, not a
    verified one. It has to be checked against the areas the shop actually drives to
    before go-live.
  \item[Units per bag, per species.] No default is assumed. Every livestock product
    needs this set before its packaging can be quoted accurately.
  \item[A local tier for dry goods and very-large items.] Only livestock has a
    local rate. If a cheaper local rate is wanted for the other two, it is a settings
    change plus one line of code --- no change to the pricing logic.
  \item[The shipping-class slugs.] The seeded translation list has to be checked
    against the slugs the store really uses. A wrong slug does not error --- those
    products silently fall through to the unclassified default.
  \item[Notices on the block cart and checkout.] Section~\ref{sec:blocknotice}. This
    matters the day anybody rebuilds those pages with blocks.
\end{description}

\section{Collection appears twice if Local Pickup is also enabled}
\label{sec:collection-clash}

The plugin offers its own collection option, and it also recognises WooCommerce's
built-in Local Pickup as collection --- both correctly remove transport while keeping
packaging payable.

If both are configured, customers see two collection options. Pick one: either untick
\emph{Offer collection from the Riverbend store} in the plugin settings, or remove
Local Pickup from the shipping zone.

\chapter{Technical reference}

This chapter is for whoever maintains the code. It is not needed to run the shop.

\section{What is in the distributed package}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4.6cm}L{10.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Path} & \textbf{Contents}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\opt{riverbend-shipping.php} & Plugin header and bootstrap. Registers the autoloader and boots the plugin.\\
\opt{includes/} & The pricing engine. No WordPress code.\\
\opt{wordpress/} & The WooCommerce adapter.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Nothing else ships. There is no \opt{vendor} directory: the plugin registers its own
PSR-4 autoloader, so Composer is a development tool only.

\section{Building a release}

From the plugin repository:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
./build-release.ps1
\end{lstlisting}

This reads the \opt{Version:} header from \opt{riverbend-shipping.php}, stages the three
runtime paths into a folder named after the plugin slug, and writes
\opt{build/riverbend-shipping-<version>.zip}. Bumping the version header is the only
step needed before cutting a new build.

\section{Development tooling}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
composer install
composer check
\end{lstlisting}

\opt{composer check} runs, in order: \opt{phpcs} (PSR-12), \opt{phpstan} at level 9
over both trees, and \opt{phpunit}. They can be run individually as \opt{composer cs},
\opt{composer stan} and \opt{composer test}.

PHPStan uses two configurations. One covers the engine; the other covers the adapter
and loads the WordPress and WooCommerce stubs. The split is deliberate --- the stubs
are deliberately \emph{not} available to the engine, which is what makes ``no
WordPress code in the engine'' something a tool can verify rather than a habit.

\section{Design rules worth preserving}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Money is integer cents.} The engine never divides money, so there is no
        rounding policy to get wrong.
  \item \textbf{Exactly one ceiling function.} Both the bag count and the cooler-box
        count go through it, and it refuses floats outright.
  \item \textbf{Rates are VAT-exclusive.} VAT is WooCommerce's job.
  \item \textbf{Purity.} The same cart plus the same settings always produces an
        identical quote. There is no clock in the engine --- heat-pack season is
        resolved to a yes or no at the boundary, before the engine is built.
  \item \textbf{The transport/packaging split follows the cost line's own definition},
        not a hand-maintained list. A new cost line lands on whichever side its
        definition puts it.
\end{itemize}

\section{The manual-adjustment filter}
\label{sec:manual}

The breakdown supports a \textsf{Manual surcharge or adjustment} line, but no pricing
rule produces one --- it is a human decision about a specific order. Something else
can set it through a filter, in integer cents:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=PHP]
add_filter( 'riverbend_shipping_manual_adjustment_cents', function ( $cents ) {
    return 6000; // R60.00 surcharge
} );
\end{lstlisting}

A manual adjustment is added as a cart fee and survives both whole-order rules in
Section~\ref{sec:step6}.

\section{Verification status}

At version 0.1.0 the plugin has been exercised in two ways:

\begin{itemize}
  \item An \textbf{automated suite} covering the engine, the adapter's cart mapping,
        the settings parsing and a set of end-to-end pipeline scenarios --- including
        the worked examples reproduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:calc}.
  \item A \textbf{27-scenario manual test round} against a staging store with a real
        product catalogue, covering every shipping class, both fulfilment choices,
        free-shipping boundaries, packaging arithmetic, notices, order storage and the
        settings screen. All 27 passed; the only finding was the block-checkout notice
        limitation in Section~\ref{sec:blocknotice}.
\end{itemize}
